Rimefall

One continent, wide and frozen in the north, narrowing to a dry waste in the south. It is dying from both ends, and the only question the game asks is who is still standing when it stops.

Two hordes have homes on this continent. In the far north, at The Rimewell, the ice-bound dead gather and walk south. On the eastern shore, at The Saltspire, dragons wake and strike inland. Neither can be bargained with, outrun forever, or worn down by attrition. A horde stops when its home is taken and not before.

The bands

The continent is read in five latitude bands, from the frozen north down through the near north, the middle lands and the near south to the far south. The bands are the map's organising idea: terrain follows them, and so does where new players arrive.

Starting locations are not spread evenly. The middle lands hold the most, the edges the fewest. That is deliberate: the edges are pressured by monsters and the middle by other players, and the crowding is roughly twice as tight in the centre as at either rim. A quiet start in the far south means the most land per faction anywhere — and the dragons within reach.

Arriving

In the Nexus you will find gateways, one for each starting location still free. Each is named for the band and terrain it leads to — Gateway to the middle lands, forest — and taking one puts you exactly there. One gateway is one location: no two factions arrive through the same one.

A gateway you have used is renamed Sealed gateway and leads nowhere. When the northern front overruns a band, its remaining gateways become Lost gateways and close: the game will not send newcomers into ground that is already gone. When every location is held or lost, new factions are refused rather than stranded in the Nexus.

Starting alliances — a default, not a pact

Factions that arrive near one another begin mutually ALLY with each other. You did not agree to this and neither did they.

Understand what ALLY means before you rely on it. It is the heaviest attitude in the game. An ally may GIVE UNIT to you, and your ally's units are not caught by your guards; theft and assassination attempts against an ally fail. It is not a nod of goodwill, it is an open gate.

It is also one-sided at any moment. Anyone may DECLARE a different attitude on any turn, without warning and without any announcement to you. A faction that assumes its opening alliances still hold, and does not check, will find out at its own expense. Check your neighbours' attitudes; do not infer them from turn one.

This applies only to the factions you started near. Everyone else begins neutral, as usual.

The northern front

The dead do not wander. They advance, in a band that creeps south as the game ages, and nothing players do slows it. Not victories, not walls, not tribute. It is the game's clock, and it will reach the middle of the map and then the southern edge whether or not anyone is ready.

Whether the front attacks in a given turn, and how hard, is a separate matter, and that part you do influence. Three things feed it: how long the game has run, how much prosperity lies within the front's reach, and how much fighting players have been doing among themselves. Wealth draws them. So does war. Early turns are quiet not because of any grace period but because the clock has barely started.

The eastern front

The dragons wake later, when the northern band has come far enough south — or immediately, if The Rimewell falls first. Success in the north brings the second act forward; it does not cancel it.

Where the north is a wall of attrition, the east is few and fast, and it strikes inland from the coast rather than advancing on a line.

Taking a source

Each source is garrisoned. Clearing the garrison stops that front permanently, and this is the only thing that does.

Taking one has two steps and they cannot be done in one turn: while its defenders hold the building you cannot enter it, so you must clear it in one turn and move in on the next. If you clear a source and then leave, its guard returns — the horde is not finished until someone is standing in its home.

Winning

While either source still stands there is no winner at all. Not a leading faction, not a provisional one. Both hordes must be ended first.

Once both are held, the throne opens, and it is settled by alliance rather than by conquest: the crown goes to the faction holding mutual ALLY from at least half of the other surviving factions. Mutual means both directions — a one-sided ALLY is a gift, not a vote.

Your opening alliances are nowhere near enough. They are small clusters by design, so no bloc can crown itself; a candidate has to win over factions who owe them nothing. And remember what a vote costs the voter: crowning someone means being ALLY to them, with everything that permits. The ballot is not free.

A tied vote crowns nobody and the game goes on. If the hordes take the continent before anyone is crowned, the game ends with no winner at all — which is the ordinary outcome if the fronts are treated as scenery.

This instance of Atlantis is a computer moderated fantasy game for any number of players. New players may join while starting locations remain, which is to say while the continent lasts.
